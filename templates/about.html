Method & limits

What an inspection tells you.

{% if engine_mode == "fusion" %}

EfficientNet feature fusion

The active engine combines 1,280 EfficientNet-B0 features, 63 DCT coefficients and 63 pHash bits in a trained MLP. The backbone is fine-tuned for facial manipulation on FF++ C23, then frozen for the feature-fusion comparison. Each receipt records the exported model hash and training manifest hash.

Video processing

Every video frame is decoded. Guarded pHash similarity can reuse a nearby anchor score, with a forced refresh at least every 12 frames. Reused frames retain their temporal weight. This approximation can miss subtle or brief edits; any reused-frame clip remains inconclusive at low scores. Uploads are limited to 3,600 frames and a 90-second processing deadline.

Evaluation and limits

The controlled T4 experiment compares the trained EfficientNet baseline on every frame, a deep-feature-only MLP, fusion on every frame, and fusion with pHash reuse. Accuracy, error rates, calibration, per-method scores and repeated end-to-end timing are recorded separately. Source-pair splits do not prove identity-disjoint generalization. Fully AI-generated video detection is a separate v2 task. Audio, lip synchronization and pulse signals are not analyzed.

A reproducible screening model

Authenfy uses the corrected Community Forensics ViT-Small FP32 model. Its immutable revision and SHA-256 are included in each receipt. Generated-image screening uses an aspect-preserving resize, a 384 × 384 center crop and CLIP normalization. A separate pinned CLIP-L facial-manipulation specialist analyzes up to four detected faces per sampled frame. YuNet supplies five landmarks; a similarity transform aligns each face to 256 × 256 before the specialist's 224 × 224 input preprocessing. This approximates the specialist's original dlib pipeline and has not been broadly validated on arbitrary uploads.

Scores and review bands

The screening score takes the maximum of the generated-image and facial-manipulation scores for each sampled frame, then averages across time. This conservative heuristic is not a calibrated likelihood that content is fake. Both component scores and face coverage appear in the evidence table. A mean score of at least 0.8 is marked “Synthetic signals detected.” A maximum score of at most 0.2 is marked “Low synthetic signals” only if all detected faces in every sampled frame were assessed. No-face, missing-specialist and partial-coverage cases are inconclusive at low scores. Other inputs, or clips with score disagreement above 0.5, are marked “Inconclusive.” These operating bands have not been calibrated on a representative deployment dataset. Every outcome requires human review.

Video evidence

Up to 16 frames are sampled uniformly over the clip. Only byte-identical decoded frames skip repeat inference. Perceptual hashes are included as descriptive evidence, never used as proof that two files are equivalent. Video aggregation weights every sampled frame equally, including duplicates.

Known blind spots

The generic image model missed all 20 manipulated crops in our small face-swap audit. This motivated adding the facial specialist. The audit contains correlated example crops and cannot establish production accuracy. Center crops can miss local edits, and temporal sampling can miss short manipulations. Audio, audiovisual synchronization, pulse signals and manipulation localization are not implemented. Compression, cropping and unfamiliar generators can change scores. No production accuracy claim is made.

Receipts and blockchain

Receipts are signed with a server-held HMAC key and stored in a local SQLite database. Verification checks receipt integrity and exact file bytes. This provides a local record, not public independent attestation. A publisher-restricted Ethereum registry is implemented and tested on an in-process development chain. Public blockchain anchoring is not active. Neither a hash nor a blockchain record proves that a depicted event is true.
